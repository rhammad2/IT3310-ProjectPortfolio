## Estimation Approach

I used a combination of bottom-up and analogous estimating. I estimated the hours each role would need based on their tasks and used reasonable hourly rates similar to our Week 10 lecture activity. This works because we already know the project's main tasks and schedule. However, the actual hours and costs could change if the project takes longer than expected.

**Labor Assumptions:**

1. I assume the project will follow the planned schedule and each role will work the estimated hours. If the project takes longer, labor costs will increase.
2. I assume the team will have enough staff to complete development and testing without extra help. If more workers or additional hours are needed, the budget will increase.

## Contingency Reserve

### Risk-Based Cost Exposure

- **Risk 3: Additional appointment data errors** – Potential cost exposure: $2,000. More errors could require extra developer hours to fix the data and test the system again.
- **Risk 4: Incomplete acceptance testing** – Potential cost exposure: $1,500. If important tests are not completed, the team may need additional testing time before launch.

**Recommended Contingency Reserve: $3,500**

This reserve covers the estimated additional costs from these two risks instead of using a general percentage.

## Budget Summary

**Total Labor Cost:** $52,725

**Total Non-Labor Cost:** $6,500

**Contingency Reserve:** $3,500

**Estimated Project Budget:** $62,725

### Largest Cost Category

Labor is the largest cost category because the RCHN project needs people to manage, develop, and test the new system. This is appropriate because the system must work correctly across all six clinics and meet their needs before launch.

### Budget Tradeoff Analysis

Before agreeing to a 10% budget reduction, I would review the project costs to see where we could save money without affecting the system's quality. A 10% reduction would mean saving about $6,273.

**Tradeoff 1:** We could reduce outside training costs by using internal staff, but this might increase their workload and delay training.

**Tradeoff 2:** We could use less expensive software or cloud services, but this could affect system performance or require more testing.

I would avoid reducing important testing or removing the contingency reserve because incorrect appointment data is already a major risk.
